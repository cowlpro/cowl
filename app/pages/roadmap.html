Roadmap

Built in order. Shipped when it's real.

COWL does one thing: it lets you get paid without your wallet showing up on-chain. Everything below either makes that easier, or makes it work in more places. We work through it top to bottom. There are no dates on this page, on purpose.

Order, not datesEach phase starts when the one before it is done. A date we might miss helps nobody.
Shipped means checkableNothing moves to Shipped until it is live on mainnet and you can try it or read it yourself.
Same rules for every featureNo custody, no pooling, keys stay in your browser. A feature that breaks that does not ship.
01

The core

Shipped

Private receive links on Robinhood Chain, working end to end and tested with real funds before launch.

Private receive linksOpen

One free signature creates your keys. Every payment lands at a fresh one-time address only you can open.

Inbox and withdrawOpen

Your browser finds your payments with your viewing key and moves them with one click. The server never learns which are yours.

Short link through the ERC-6538 registryOpen

Pay a wallet address directly and the money still lands at a fresh address. The short link does name your wallet to whoever you share it with.

ETH, $COWL and all 50 Robinhood stock tokensOpen

Plus any ERC-20 by address. Token payments carry a little ETH so the receiver can withdraw.

Verified Router with no owner and no feeBlockscout

About 80 lines. Holds nothing, can be upgraded by nobody.

03

COWL Names

Shipped

The 0x short link is your wallet address. Names fix that: a short link that is easy to share and still keeps your wallet out of it.

Claim a name like cowl.pro/mayaClaim

It points to your receive keys, not your wallet, so payers learn nothing about what you hold. Payment requests and the Pay me privately button use it too.

For $COWL holdersLive

Claiming needs 100,000 $COWL in the claiming wallet, checked once at claim. One name per wallet. Paying a name needs nothing.

Plainly publicBy design

Names are public records. The claiming wallet is not published: the server keeps only a salted hash so that wallet can change or release its name.

04

Withdraw without ETH

Building now

Today, moving a token payment out of its one-time address uses a little ETH the sender attached. A relayer removes that step.

RelayerIn development

Withdrawals that do not need ETH in the one-time address first. Same rule as the Router: it never holds your funds.

05

For teams

Planned

Once single payments are effortless, the next step is paying many people, and proving it.

Private payrollPlanned

Payment receiptsPlanned

A receipt for the sender, and a way for the receiver to prove a payment arrived without revealing their main wallet.

Not on the roadmap

Things people ask for that we will not promise.

  • Hidden amounts or senders. Not without zero-knowledge proofs and an independent audit. Until then, they stay public and we say so.
  • Pools, mixing or custody. Money goes straight to an address only the receiver controls. That does not change.
  • Yield, staking or rewards for holding $COWL. The app works the same with or without the token.

When something ships, it moves up this page and gets announced on @cowlhq. What stays public and what does not: Transparency.

COWL is open-source software, not a bank or broker. Unaudited; use at your own risk. This page describes plans, not promises of dates or outcomes.
